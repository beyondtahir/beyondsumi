# Hooks, endings and dramatic sound

## Opening

In the first beat, give a clear subject, a tension or question, and something visibly happening. A title, if used, is two to six words and on screen for at most three seconds, and it does not cover the first line of narration.

## Ending

Show the consequence, and let the final image or line have room to land. Plan its animation and sound like any other shot: a static afterthought ends a film badly. Let the music resolve and the sound decay.

## Sound follows the picture

For each important sound, note the frame, what visibly causes it and why it matters. After the voice is retimed, move each sound back onto the real event: cause, impact, reaction.

- **Keep voice, music, room sound and effects separate,** so each can be changed.
- **Lower the music under the voice,** rather than weakening every impact.
- **Make the sound do what the words say.** When the story says the drums stopped, stop them, and let the silence carry the line.
- **Build real events:** a gate strike is a low hit, a crack of wood and falling debris, not one rumble. An arrow volley has an onset, air and selected impacts, not one whoosh.
- **Dramatic does not always mean loud.** A quiet ending often works better with less.

Meters and cue lists do not prove how a mix feels. A person must listen.

# Preserve the script; resolve duration before production

Use this contract for any user when writing or revising a timed film. It governs content and runtime decisions; it does not prescribe the style, voice, language, editing rhythm or number of scenes.

## Establish what must survive

Keep the approved spoken wording, required visible text and essential visual story beats distinct. Preserve their meaning, causal links and ending. Stage directions are not automatically voiceover, and unchanged spoken lines do not prove that the visual story is complete. A requested runtime alone is not permission to delete approved content. Rewrite or shorten only within the user's actual authorization.

Before paid narration, images or other timing-dependent media, estimate the complete cut: speech in the intended language/delivery, readable text holds, consequential actions, transitions, pauses and an ending. Use a read-through or suitable pacing estimate and a rough beat timeline. Account for speech and action that can overlap without double-counting them; allow dedicated time when they cannot. State an estimate or range, not an exact duration inferred from word count alone. Silent films still need reading and action time.

## Resolve a real conflict

Compare the estimate with the user's requested runtime/range. When approved content cannot fit at an intelligible, readable pace, present both concrete options:

- The full-content version, its estimated duration and what needs the extra time.
- A proposed shorter script/beat edit that fits the requested target, with the actual wording or beats changed or removed made clear.

Ask which option the user wants **before shortening approved content or materially extending a fixed request**. Continue independent preparation while that choice is pending; do not spend on media that depends on the unresolved edit. This is in addition to the [director-plan approval](../../sumi-director/SKILL.md) for each new film. Combine both decisions in one concrete approval request when possible.

Apply existing authorization instead of asking again. If the user already approved this concrete script/runtime option, carry out that choice. Broad earlier permission to extend does not override a newer instruction to present the actual runtime options and wait. If the user already authorizes an edit to fit a firm cap, perform that edit within scope and disclose it. A later instruction can override an older duration. Record the selected script/beat revision, intended duration and relevant user choice in the project's existing plan; do not rely on a personal preference from another project.

## Check the actual take and final cut

Measure the decoded narration after generation and compare the actual words with the selected script. Alignment timestamps assist placement but do not establish media duration or pronunciation. Rebuild timing around the real take and required visible actions. Do not force a fit through speech acceleration, clipped words, removed phrases, unreadable text or omitted causal beats. If the actual take creates a new material conflict outside existing authorization, resolve it by the same choice above before further dependent generation.

Keep the original take and receipts. Update captions, action cues, music/effects and the final duration together; retain breaths, intelligible phrase boundaries and the ending. Check full playback and the encoded result against the selected script/beat revision. Record measured duration and any remaining content, timing or listening limitation. A technical duration check alone does not prove the complete story was delivered.

# Write the story before making the film

Use the current assistant's writing ability. This step does not require a separate story API, new image, narration take or render. Work in the user's requested language and script, including Roman Urdu when requested; do not silently switch language. Follow the user's idea, facts, tone and must-keep actions rather than replacing the premise with your own story.

## First establish length and how it is told

If missing, first ask **“How long would you like the video to be?”** Offer a simple duration choice or accept their answer. Then ask **“Would you like a narrator telling the story, characters speaking, or a mixture of both?”** A clear existing answer needs no repeat question. If they already supplied a story, ask whether to preserve it or help improve it; do not rewrite approved wording automatically. For a rough idea, ask whether they want help writing the story first. Reuse known audience, mood and language; collect only the missing decisions.

## Develop an engaging story with the user

Start with an understandable hook: a striking action, unsettling detail, emotional problem, unanswered question or intriguing contrast that the story actually resolves. Give the audience a reason to care promptly. Avoid a generic greeting, a slow explanation before anything happens or a promise the ending cannot answer.

Build a clear chain of cause and consequence. Each beat changes the situation, reveals useful information or develops the character's feelings. Give important actions a setup and visible consequence. Remove redundant explanation and repeated actions; keep meaningful breaths, reactions and suspense. Pace comes from useful change, not constant shouting or fast speech. A simple story can remain simple; do not add a twist, violence, moral or extra character without a reason and the user's acceptance.

For factual stories, separate supported history from dramatized dialogue and reconstruction. Do not invent facts to make a hook stronger. For supplied recordings, use their actual words, emotions and duration under [the recording workflow](../../sumi/references/supplied-narration.md).

## Write a performance, not a flat reading

Write narration so a person can speak it naturally. Choose words, sentence lengths and pauses for the feeling: wonder, tenderness, urgency, fear, grief, relief or resolve. Mark restrained performance notes separately from words that will be spoken. Direct changes in feeling across the story; do not put an identical dramatic pause after every sentence or pad the film with silence.

For dialogue, label the speaker and write purposeful lines. Keep the narrator from repeating a line the character already says. Record the intended emotion and reply/reaction; [dialogue and lip-sync](../../sumi-animator/SKILL.md) later map the approved line to an actual voice and visible speaker. Provider delivery controls differ; never send unsupported acting notes as spoken text by accident.

## Review the script before artwork

Show the proposed title, full narration/dialogue, a short beat outline, tone and estimated duration. Identify any changes to supplied writing. Check the hook, causal clarity, emotional progression, required actions, ending/payoff and spoken readability. The assistant recommends improvements, the user decides material changes.

If the complete story exceeds 30 seconds or the requested target, follow [the duration contract](script-duration.md): present the full estimate and a concrete shorter alternative, then wait for the user's selection. Do not independently cut or rush the story. Continue to [direction](../../sumi-director/SKILL.md) only after script review and record the user's scope approval before production.

Sound is part of the story treatment from the beginning: identify the changing musical mood, audible setting and consequential action cues. The assistant interprets those needs into a cue plan; the sound tools execute that authored plan. The engine is not a separate language model that can infer every sound from prose. Use [sound coverage](../../sumi-sound/SKILL.md) before production and export.

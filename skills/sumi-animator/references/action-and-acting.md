# Action, physics and acting

Read this before ordering any action sheet. Check the real drawings, because a tool passing does not prove the anatomy is right.

## Plan the whole action

- **Phases:** start pose, anticipation, movement, contact, reaction, settle. Review the way in and the way out, not only the middle.
- **Whole figures:** draw complete poses, never separate limbs. A walking sheet does not prove a kneel, a reach or a fall works, so each action gets its own.
- **Interaction:** when two people or a person and a prop connect, draw them together, from one sheet. Plan who holds what, and when it changes hands.
- **Fix the drawing:** do not hide a wrong pose with a crop, dust or camera movement.

## Check the body

| Part | Look for |
|---|---|
| **Legs and weight** | Left and right alternate, knees bend the right way, hips sit over what supports them, and weight moves from foot to foot |
| **Feet** | The whole sole sits on the ground. A planted foot stays planted, and does not slide |
| **Walks** | Contact, down, passing and up. The same leg does not lead twice. The body faces where it travels |
| **Hands and props** | The object is really gripped at the start, the contact and the end. The thumb is on the correct side |
| **Layers** | The near arm and leg are in front. Nearer objects cross in front of the figure |
| **Size** | A figure stays one size across sheets, unless it moves in depth |

Any wrong bend, sliding foot, loose hand or broken silhouette means the action is not ready. Redraw that action, and keep the shots that work.

## Acting

Before drawing, write what the character knows, feels and hides in this shot, and one clear thing that shows it: eyes shift toward a sound, fingers tighten, a hand hesitates.

- **Give the feeling a shape:** the start, the peak, the reaction and the settle, timed to the voice.
- **Match the emotion:** fear stops the breath and tenses the fingers. Grief lowers the eyes and the shoulders. Not every feeling needs the same breathing and blinking.
- **Stillness can be acting.** Record why in `hold_reason`.
- **Do not repeat a loop** through every emotion, or blink on a metronome.

## Continuity

Keep a small table for anything the story depends on:

| Shot | State coming in | What happens on screen | State going out |
|---|---|---|---|
| s1 | Hands empty | She receives the letter | Sealed letter in her right hand |
| s2 | Sealed letter in her right hand | She opens it | Open letter in both hands |

Track who knows what, injuries, clothes, which hand holds what, open or closed, route, weather and time of day. Consequences carry to later shots. An open letter never reseals itself, and a time jump must read clearly on screen.

## Mouths

For a character who speaks on screen, draw closed, open and rounded mouth shapes on the character's own sheet, then time them to the real voice. The narrator does not move anyone's mouth. Say so when timing is only approximate.

## Mistakes the tools can miss

Look for these yourself, because the checks once passed all of them:

- **A body part that disappears:** feet, hands or a head that stop being drawn partway through an action. If later drawings lose the feet, cut closer so the frame hides them, or redraw.
- **A size jump inside a shot:** never change a character's scale between layers in the same shot. If the scream needs to feel bigger, cut to a closer shot.
- **Looking the wrong way:** a character who turns to something must end up facing it.
- **An action with no ending:** if a shot cuts before the boat reaches the table, end the shot on a moment that is complete, such as her looking at it, and make the next shot follow from that.
- **Hair that leaves the head:** tie hair and ribbons to the head's own turn, not to a guess.
